Leadership Fluency: How to Lead When AI Does (Almost) Everything Else

A daylong workshop that helps leaders untangle the four roles of leadership, and consciously choose between leading, managing, directing, and coaching to move people and work forward.

If you’d like a deeper dive into the framework, I wrote an introductory article on my blog.

Why This Workshop?

Exploring the four modes of leadership gave me the clarity I’d been missing. It’s a fantastic framework, I can’t wait to begin applying it within our org.

When you step into a leadership role you take on four distinct areas of responsibility: holding and communicating the vision, defining outcomes, running the business, and developing talent.

In practice, this all blends together. It's hard to see where your energy really goes, or which of the four areas you spend the most time in.

The leader focuses on vision without clarifying clear targets, the director focuses on outcomes but doesn’t connect them with the larger purpose, the operational leader excells at process but falls short when it comes to people, and the people-oriented leader is great at building trust but can’t seem to drive results.

Whatever your strengths and leadership comfort zone, this workshop helps you map your complete leadership landscape to get clear on what you spend time on and what could use more focus. With that clarity, you can make conscious choices about how you want to lead rather than operating from habit or overwhelm.

This workshop is designed for folks who have at least some leadership experience under their belt, whether in a management track or IC track (staff, principal, etc.).

Pre-work

20 - 30 minute reflective practice to surface the dimensions of your current leadership work.

Workshop 1: The Four Roles of Leadership

Leadership is more complex than any single style or approach can contain. In this foundational workshop, you’ll be introduced to the Leadership Fluency Framework—a map of four distinct roles that every leader navigates: LEAD, DIRECT, MANAGE, and COACH.

We explore these four roles as distinct stances, each with its own mindset, posture, and conversation type, each connecting different dimensions of organizational life.

Through reflection, small group work, and somatic exploration, you’ll develop an intuitive feel for what each role looks and feels like in practice. You'll also have a chance to apply this to your own leadership practice to understand what each role looks like for you.

You leave with a clear picture of your current leadership shape—and a sense of where you want it to go.

Workshop 2: Leadership Fluency in Action

Now that you are familiar with the roles, Workshop 2 gives you an opportunity to apply them. You’ll bring the framework into contact with the real challenges, conversations, and organizational dynamics you navigate every day.

We’ll explore how the shape of your own leadership, learn how conversation types can help you read a situation, and practice consciously choosing the role(s) a situation calls for.

We’ll look at how pairs of roles lock together to create an organizational model that drives strategy, execution, structure, relationships, and culture, as well as how to manage the tensions between them so your organization stays in motion.

We’ll also zoom out to the team level, examining how leadership fluency shows up collectively and how your leadership team can work with the framework together.

The workshop closes with reflection and a practical next-steps design, so you leave with real commitments for where you're taking your practice.

For Teams and Companies

Exploring the four modes of leadership gave me the clarity I’d been missing. It’s a fantastic framework, I can’t wait to begin applying it within our org.

Leadership Fluency can be run in-house for leadership teams, providing both individual clarity and collective insight into your team’s leadership patterns.

Leaders gain clarity on their own leadership landscape—where their time goes, where their blindspots are, and how they want to lead. When a team goes through this together they also get to see how leadership effort is distributed across the entire team—where you’re collectively over-indexed and where gaps exist that no one is covering. This reveals systemic patterns that are invisible when looking at individual leadership practices alone.

Teams leave with a shared framework and language for different types of leadership, allowing them to be more intentional about how they lead and shape work.

The in-house version can be customized for your organizational context—using your actual challenges, organiszational structures, and team dynamics as the material for mapping and reflection.
